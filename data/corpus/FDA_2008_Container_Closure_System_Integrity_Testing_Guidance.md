# DOC_ID: FDA-CCIT-2008
# TITLE: FDA Guidance for Industry: Container and Closure System Integrity Testing in Lieu of Sterility Testing as a Component of the Stability Protocol for Sterile Products
# SOURCE: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (CDER/CBER/CVM/ORA), February 2008 (Public Domain, 17 U.S.C. 105)

## Section 1: FDA Preference for Deterministic Container Closure Integrity Testing (CCIT)
In the 2008 FDA Guidance on Container and Closure System Integrity Testing, the agency allows validated physical/chemical **container closure integrity tests (CCIT)** to replace end-product sterility testing on stability batches (after initial time-zero sterility release).
- **Deterministic Leak Test Methods**: Rely on physicochemical phenomena that follow predictable physical laws (helium mass spectrometry, vacuum pressure decay, high-voltage leak detection, and laser headspace analysis). They are non-destructive, quantitative, and reproducible.
- **Probabilistic Leak Test Methods**: Rely on stochastic events such as microbial immersion challenge or methylene blue dye ingress. Because probabilistic methods destroy samples and exhibit high operator variance, FDA encourages validated deterministic methods.

## Section 2: Critical Leak Rate and Maximum Allowable Leakage Limit (MALL)
For rigid non-porous sterile containers (glass vials with elastomeric stoppers and prefilled syringes), the critical leak threshold (Kirsch et al. correlation cited in FDA sterile packaging review) defines the **Maximum Allowable Leakage Limit (MALL)**:
- **Critical Leak Rate**: Approximately **6 x 10^-6 mbar·L/s** (helium mass spectrometry equivalent), corresponding to a nominal micro-channel defect diameter of **0.1 to 0.3 micrometers (µm)**.
- Below `6 x 10^-6 mbar·L/s` (defect diameter `< 0.2 µm`), neither liquid leakage nor microbial ingress occurs.

## Section 3: Sensitivity Comparison of Deterministic CCIT Methods
1. **Helium Mass Spectrometry**: Detects leak rates down to **10^-9 to 10^-11 mbar·L/s** (sub-0.1 µm defects across the MALL boundary). Ideal for vial capping qualification and ultra-cold (`-80°C`) storage validation.
2. **Vacuum Decay (ASTM F2338)**: Detects **1.0 to 5.0 µm** defects non-destructively across liquid and lyophilized vials.
3. **High-Voltage Leak Detection (HVLD)**: Detects **1.0 to 2.0 µm** defects at 100% inline production speeds for conductive liquid parenterals inside glass or polymer barrels.
4. **Laser Headspace Analysis (FMS)**: Measures headspace oxygen (`O2`), moisture (`H2O`), or carbon dioxide (`CO2`) ingress down to **0.3 to 1.0 µm** equivalent leak rates.
