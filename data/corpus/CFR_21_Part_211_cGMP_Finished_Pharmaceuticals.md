# DOC_ID: CFR-211-CGMP
# TITLE: 21 CFR Part 211 — Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) for Finished Pharmaceuticals
# SOURCE: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, Code of Federal Regulations Title 21 Part 211

## Section 1: Quality Control Unit Responsibilities (21 CFR 211.22)
Under 21 CFR 211.22, every pharmaceutical manufacturer must establish an independent **Quality Control Unit (QCU)** with the authority and responsibility to:
- Approve or reject all drug product components, drug product containers, closures, in-process materials, packaging material, labeling, and finished drug products.
- Review production batch records to assure that no errors have occurred or, if errors have occurred, that they have been fully investigated under 21 CFR 211.192.
- Approve or reject all procedures or specifications impacting the identity, strength, quality, and purity of the drug product.

## Section 2: Control of Components, Drug Product Containers, and Closures (21 CFR 211.80 - 211.94)
- **Quarantine and Sampling (21 CFR 211.82 & 211.84)**: Upon receipt, each lot of components, drug product containers, and closures must be withheld from use in quarantine until sampled, tested, or examined and released by the Quality Control Unit. At least **one specific identity test** must be conducted on each lot of incoming components even if a supplier Certificate of Analysis (CoA) is accepted for other attributes.
- **Retesting and Reserve Samples (21 CFR 211.87 & 211.170)**: Approved components and closures are retested after storage exposure to air, heat, or humidity. Reserve samples of each lot of active ingredient and finished drug product (at least **twice the quantity** necessary for all required quality control tests except sterility and pyrogens) must be retained for **at least 1 year after the expiration date** of the drug product.

## Section 3: Batch Production Records and Out-of-Specification Investigations (21 CFR 211.188 & 211.192)
Every unexplained discrepancy (including a batch yield falling outside maximum/minimum percentage limits) or the failure of a batch or any of its components to meet any specification must be thoroughly investigated under **21 CFR 211.192**, whether or not the batch has already been distributed. The investigation must extend to other batches of the same drug product and other products that may have been associated with the specific failure or discrepancy, and must produce a written record including conclusions and CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action) follow-up.
