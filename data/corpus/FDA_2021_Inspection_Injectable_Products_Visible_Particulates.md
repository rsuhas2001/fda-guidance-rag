# DOC_ID: FDA-PARTICULATES-2021
# TITLE: FDA Draft Guidance for Industry: Inspection of Injectable Products for Visible Particulates
# SOURCE: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (CDER/CBER/CVM), December 2021

## Section 1: Classification of Visible Particulates (Intrinsic, Extrinsic, and Inherent)
To protect patients from capillary occlusion, phlebitis, granulomas, and immune reactions, the FDA categorizes visible particulates in injectable drug products into three classes:
1. **Extrinsic Particulates**: Foreign materials that are **not part of the formulation, package, or assembly process**, such as hair, clothing fibers, insect parts, paint flakes, or environmental dust. Extrinsic particles represent a sporadic cGMP contamination failure and carry high sterility/microbiological risk.
2. **Intrinsic Particulates**: Materials that **originate from within the manufacturing equipment or primary container closure system**, such as glass lamellae (delamination flakes), elastomeric stopper fragments/shavings, stainless-steel wear particles from filling pumps, or coalesced silicone oil droplets.
3. **Inherent Particulates**: Materials that are **known characteristics of the specific drug product formulation** itself (such as protein agglomerates in certain biologic formulations, emulsions, or liposomal suspensions). Inherent particulates are only acceptable if characterized and explicitly permitted in the approved product specification.

## Section 2: 100% Visual Inspection and Compendial AQL Acceptance Sampling
- **100% Manufacturing Inspection**: Every finished injectable container must undergo 100% visual inspection (manual human inspection at **2,000 to 3,750 lux** illumination for at least **5 seconds against a black background and 5 seconds against a white background**, or validated semi-automated/automated camera inspection) to reject defective units.
- **AQL Batch Release Sampling (USP <790> / ANSI/ASQ Z1.4)**: Following 100% inspection, a statistically representative sample of accepted units is inspected under General Inspection Level II. The Acceptable Quality Limit (AQL) must not exceed **0.65%** for visible particulates, and **0.0%** for critical defects that compromise sterility or container closure integrity (such as cracks or incomplete crimp seals).
- **Knapp Methodology Qualification**: Automated visual inspection (AVI) machines and human inspectors must be qualified using calibrated particle standard sets across a range of particle sizes (`50 µm to 500 µm`) to establish the **Rejection Zone Efficiency (RZE)** against the probabilistic **70% detection threshold** ( typically around `100–150 µm` for clear solutions in clear glass vials).
