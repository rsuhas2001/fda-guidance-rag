# DOC_ID: USP-381-ELASTOMERS
# TITLE: USP <381> Elastomeric Components in Injectable Pharmaceutical Product Packaging/Delivery Systems
# SOURCE: United States Pharmacopeia (USP) General Chapter <381>

## Section 1: Classification of Elastomeric Closures (Type I vs. Type II)
USP <381> classifies elastomeric closures used in parenteral containers into two functional categories based on coating/lamination:
- **Type I Closures**: Uncoated elastomeric components (such as standard bromobutyl, chlorobutyl, or polyisoprene rubber vial stoppers and syringe plungers) where the rubber compound directly contacts the pharmaceutical formulation.
- **Type II Closures**: Elastomeric components coated or laminated with an inert barrier layer (such as polytetrafluoroethylene [PTFE], ethylene tetrafluoroethylene [ETFE] fluoropolymer film, or cross-linked silicone coating) designed to reduce interaction, adsorption, and leachables migration between the elastomer base and sensitive drug formulations (such as biologics, mRNA lipid nanoparticles, and oncology parenterals).

## Section 2: Biological, Physicochemical, and Functionality Testing Requirements
All elastomeric closures intended for injectable drug products must pass three tiers of qualification testing under USP <381>:

### 1. Biological Reactivity Tests
- **In Vitro Biological Reactivity (USP <87>)**: Elution, agar diffusion, or direct contact cytotoxicity testing using L-929 mammalian fibroblast cells. Every elastomeric formulation must meet the requirements of USP <87> (Grade <= 2 for agar diffusion/direct contact, and Grade 0 for elution test).
- **In Vivo Biological Reactivity (USP <88>)**: Required if the component fails or shows reactivity beyond USP <87> criteria, evaluating systemic injection, intracutaneous reactivity, and implantation.

### 2. Physicochemical Tests on Aqueous Extracts (Autoclave Extraction at 121°C for 30 Minutes)
Aqueous extracts prepared at a surface area of 100 cm² per 200 mL purified water must satisfy strict acceptance limits:
- **Turbidity (Nephelometric)**: <= 6 NTU for Type I closures; <= 6 NTU for Type II closures.
- **Color of Extract**: Not more intensely colored than standard matching fluid.
- **Acidity or Alkalinity**: Not more than **0.3 mL of 0.01 N NaOH** or **0.8 mL of 0.01 N HCl** required per 20 mL of extract to reach neutrality with bromothymol blue indicator.
- **Absorbance (UV 220–360 nm)**: Maximum absorbance <= **0.2 AU** for Type I closures, or matched to the baseline profile of the qualified elastomer formula.
- **Reducing Substances**: Difference in 0.01 N sodium thiosulfate titration volume <= **3.0 mL** for Type I and <= **3.0 mL** for Type II closures.
- **Extractable Heavy Metals / Elemental Impurities**: Evaluated per USP <232>/<233> and Extractable Zinc (**<= 5 ppm [5 µg/mL]** in the aqueous extract for zinc-cured halobutyl elastomers).
- **Volatile Sulfides**: Must show no blackening of lead acetate paper larger than the 50 µg sulfide reference standard.

### 3. Functionality Tests for Vial Stoppers
Noticeable mechanical functionality tests (required unless waived for non-punctured plunger stoppers):
- **Penetrability**: Using a lubricated 21-gauge hypodermic needle (outer diameter 0.8 mm) at 200 mm/min, the puncture force for each of 10 stoppers must **not exceed 10 N (1.0 kgf)**.
- **Fragmentation (Coring)**: Across 48 total punctures (12 vials punctured 4 times each with a 21-gauge needle), the total number of visible elastomeric fragments retained on a 50-µm filter must be **<= 5 fragments** for butyl/halobutyl closures.
- **Self-Sealing Capacity**: Applicable to multi-dose containers. After 10 punctures with a 21-gauge needle and immersion in 0.1% methylene blue under 27 kPa vacuum (73 kPa absolute pressure) for 10 minutes followed by 30 minutes at atmospheric pressure, **zero out of 10 vials** may show any trace of dye ingress.
