# DOC_ID: FDA-OOS-2006
# TITLE: FDA Guidance for Industry: Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production
# SOURCE: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (CDER), October 2006

## Section 1: Two-Phase OOS Investigation Framework
In accordance with 21 CFR 211.192 and the landmark ***United States v. Barr Laboratories*** ruling, all Out-of-Specification (OOS) laboratory results during raw material, packaging component, in-process, or finished product testing must undergo a structured two-phase investigation:
1. **Phase I (Laboratory Investigation)**: Assesses the accuracy of the laboratory data before discarding any test preparations or composite solutions. The analyst and laboratory supervisor verify:
   - Whether a clear **assignable laboratory error** occurred (such as instrument malfunction, power outage, dilution calculation error, spilled sample solution, or failed system suitability).
   - If a documented, assignable laboratory error is confirmed, the original OOS result is invalidated and the sample is re-analyzed.
   - **Prohibition on "Testing into Compliance"**: In the absence of a documented laboratory error, the original OOS result **cannot be invalidated** simply by running repeat tests and averaging passing results with the failing result.
2. **Phase II (Full-Scale Manufacturing & Laboratory Investigation)**: Initiated whenever Phase I finds no assignable laboratory error. Includes a thorough review of manufacturing batch records, packaging process parameters, sampling procedures, and retesting of the original sample using a predefined protocol (specifying the exact number of replicate determinations and statistical acceptance rules).

## Section 2: Rules for Averaging, Outlier Tests, and Resampling
- **Averaging**: Averaging replicate aliquots from a single homogeneous preparation is valid for optical rotation or HPLC precision, but averaging separate individual dosage units or separate vial stopper extractions to mask an individual out-of-spec unit is prohibited because it hides batch variability.
- **Outlier Tests (USP <1010>)**: Statistical outlier tests (such as Dixon's Q-test or Grubbs' test) **cannot be used to invalidate a chemical assay or impurity OOS result**, though they may be permitted for biological assays with inherently high variability if pre-specified in the USP monograph.
