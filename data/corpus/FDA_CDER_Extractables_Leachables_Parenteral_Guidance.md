# DOC_ID: FDA-EL-PODP
# TITLE: FDA CDER Regulatory Science & PQRI Thresholds: Extractables and Leachables in Parenteral and Ophthalmic Drug Products
# SOURCE: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (CDER) Public Regulatory Guidance & PQRI PODP Public Recommendations (Public Domain, 17 U.S.C. 105)

## Section 1: Regulatory Definitions of Extractables versus Leachables
Under FDA CDER packaging review standards (21 CFR 211.94 and 21 CFR 314.50(d)(1)(ii)(a)):
- **Extractables**: Organic and inorganic chemical species that can be released from a pharmaceutical container closure component (such as elastomeric vial stoppers, prefilled syringe plungers, or plastic ampules) under laboratory extraction conditions using multiple solvents and exaggerated temperatures/durations.
- **Leachables**: Chemical species that migrate from primary or secondary packaging components directly into the drug product formulation under normal conditions of storage and clinical use over the labeled shelf life.

## Section 2: Safety Concern Threshold (SCT) and Analytical Evaluation Threshold (AET)
For parenteral and ophthalmic drug products (PODP), FDA reviewers apply established dose-based toxicological thresholds:
- **Safety Concern Threshold (SCT)**: **1.5 micrograms/day (µg/day)** for an individual organic leachable. Below 1.5 µg/day, organic leachables generally pose negligible risk of carcinogenicity or genotoxicity, unless the chemical belongs to the high-potency **Cohort of Concern** (N-nitrosamines, polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons [PAHs], or 2-mercaptobenzothiazole [2-MBT]).
- **Qualification Threshold (QT)**: **5.0 micrograms/day (µg/day)** for a non-genotoxic organic leachable; compounds above 5.0 µg/day require toxicological risk assessment based on Permitted Daily Exposure (PDE).

### Analytical Evaluation Threshold (AET) Formula
`AET (µg/mL) = (SCT [1.5 µg/day] / Maximum Daily Dose [mL/day]) / Uncertainty Factor (UF)`
Using a typical chromatographic Uncertainty Factor (`UF = 2.0`), a parenteral drug with a maximum daily dose of `3.0 mL/day` has an AET of `(1.5 / 3.0) / 2.0 = 0.25 µg/mL`.

## Section 3: Orthogonal Analytical Screening Methods
FDA CMC submissions for injectable packaging require four complementary analytical techniques:
1. **Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)**: Headspace Gas Chromatography / Mass Spectrometry (`HS-GC/MS`).
2. **Semi-Volatile Organic Compounds (SVOCs)**: Direct-injection `GC/MS` for vulcanization accelerators, antioxidants (BHT, Irganox), and plasticizers.
3. **Non-Volatile Organic Compounds (NVOCs)**: Ultra-High-Performance Liquid Chromatography coupled with High-Resolution Mass Spectrometry (`UHPLC-PDA-HRMS`).
4. **Elemental Impurities**: Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (`ICP-MS`) under **FDA / ICH Q3D(R2)** guidelines screening for catalytic metals and packaging leachables (such as Si, B, Al, Ba, and Zn).
