# TITLE: 21 CFR Part 7 — Enforcement Policy (Drug Product Recalls) & 21 CFR 314.81 Field Alert Reports (FARs)
# SOURCE: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 21 CFR Part 7 & 21 CFR 314.81(b)(1)

## Section 1: FDA Recall Classifications (Class I, Class II, and Class III)
Under 21 CFR 7.3(m), the FDA assigns a numerical designation (Class I, II, or III) to indicate the relative degree of health hazard presented by a recalled drug or combination product:
- **Class I Recall**: A situation in which there is a **reasonable probability** that the use of, or exposure to, a violative product will cause **serious adverse health consequences or death** (e.g., microbial contamination of a sterile injectable product, glass particulate delamination in an IV solution, wrong active ingredient, or superpotent/subpotent life-saving parenteral).
- **Class II Recall**: A situation in which use of, or exposure to, a violative product may cause **temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences** or where the probability of serious adverse health consequences is remote (e.g., sub-potent non-life-threatening drug or out-of-trend leachable impurity).
- **Class III Recall**: A situation in which use of, or exposure to, a violative product is **not likely to cause adverse health consequences** (e.g., minor outer carton labeling typo or slight packaging discoloration that does not affect stability or dosing).

## Section 2: NDA/ANDA Field Alert Report (FAR) 3-Working-Day Mandate (21 CFR 314.81)
Holders of approved New Drug Applications (NDAs) and Abbreviated New Drug Applications (ANDAs) must submit a **Field Alert Report (Form FDA 3331a)** to the jurisdictional FDA district office **within 3 working days** of receiving information concerning any distributed drug product that exhibits:
1. Any incident that causes the drug product or its labeling to be mistaken for, or applied to, another article;
2. Any **bacteriological contamination**, or any significant chemical, physical, or other change or deterioration in the distributed drug product; or
3. Any **failure of one or more distributed batches to meet the specification** established for it in the application (including stability failures or container closure integrity failures discovered during post-release stability testing).
For Biologics License Applications (BLAs), an analogous **Biological Product Deviation Report (BPDR)** must be submitted under **21 CFR 600.14** within **45 calendar days** of discovery.
