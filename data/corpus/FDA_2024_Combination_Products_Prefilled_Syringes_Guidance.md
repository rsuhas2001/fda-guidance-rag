# DOC_ID: FDA-PFS-2024
# TITLE: FDA Guidance: Technical Considerations for Pen, Jet, and Related Injectors and Prefilled Syringes (21 CFR Part 4 & ISO 11040)
# SOURCE: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (CDRH/CDER/CBER) & ISO 11040-4 / ISO 11608-1

## Section 1: Regulatory Framework for Drug-Device Combination Products
Prefilled syringes (PFS) and autoinjectors are regulated as **single-entity drug-device combination products** under **21 CFR Part 3** and **21 CFR Part 4**. Manufacturers must comply with streamlined current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) requirements integrating both drug cGMPs (**21 CFR Parts 210/211**) and medical device Quality System Regulation / Quality Management System Regulation (**21 CFR Part 820 / ISO 13485**), alongside **ISO 14971** for medical device risk management.

## Section 2: Critical Essential Performance Requirements (EPRs) for Prefilled Syringes
FDA submissions (IND, NDA, BLA, and 510(k)) for prefilled syringes and autoinjectors must verify Essential Performance Requirements (EPRs) across the entire shelf life (time zero through end of shelf life, including after simulated shipping per ISTA 3A / ASTM D4169):
1. **Break-Loose and Glide Force**:
   - **Break-Loose Force**: The peak initial force required to overcome static friction between the elastomeric plunger stopper and the glass or cyclic olefin polymer (COP) syringe barrel. Typical specification limit is **<= 15 N** (often 5 N to 12 N).
   - **Glide Force (Dynamic Sustaining Force)**: The average and maximum force required to maintain plunger movement at a constant speed (typically 100 to 300 mm/min). Typical specification limit is **<= 20 N** for manual injection (or matched to autoinjector spring force curves with a minimum 2x safety margin).
2. **Dose Accuracy and Deliverable Volume**: Must meet pharmacopeial deliverable volume limits (USP <697>) and ISO 11608-1 dose accuracy across cold (5°C), ambient (20-25°C), and warm (40°C) operating environments.
3. **Needle Shield Pull-Off Force (RNS / Rigid Needle Shield)**: The removal force of the elastomeric needle shield or Rigid Needle Shield must remain between **4 N and 25 N** throughout stability so patients with dexterity limitations (such as rheumatoid arthritis) can remove the cap without damaging the needle tip.
4. **Flange Breakage Resistance and Luer Cone Integrity**: Evaluated per **ISO 11040-4** (glass barrels) and **ISO 11040-6** (plastic barrels), ensuring syringe finger flanges withstand axial forces of at least **40 N to 50 N** without fracture.

## Section 3: Biologics Compatibility with Tungsten and Adhesive Residues
During the forming of staked-needle glass prefilled syringes, a **tungsten pin** is traditionally used to create the bore of the syringe tip, and UV-curable acrylic adhesives secure the stainless-steel cannula.
- **Tungsten-Induced Protein Aggregation**: Trace soluble tungsten species (tungstate polyanions, typically > 500 ppb) leached from the syringe tip cone can oxidize or denature therapeutic monoclonal antibodies and therapeutic proteins, leading to sub-visible particles (2–10 µm and >= 10 µm per USP <788>).
- **Mitigation**: For tungsten-sensitive biologics, manufacturers specify **low-tungsten (< 50 ppb)** or **tungsten-free** syringe barrels formed with ceramic or platinum-alloy pins, paired with fluoropolymer-laminated bromobutyl plunger stoppers (e.g., FluroTec-style barrier films) to prevent both silicone oil displacement and elastomer extractables.
